Recipe

JOLLOF-RICE

Jollof rice is a vibrant, one-pot West African masterpiece made by simmering long-grain parboiled rice in a rich, deeply seasoned sauce of blended tomatoes, red bell peppers, onions, and spicy scotch bonnet chilies. Infused with aromatic spices like curry powder, thyme, and bay leaves, the grains absorb a striking reddish-orange hue and a complex balance of savory depth and fiery warmth. Cooked under a tight seal to trap steam, it often develops a prized, slightly charred bottom layer that infuses the entire pot with a signature smoky aroma. Far more than just a meal, this iconic dish is a symbol of celebration, community, and intense cultural pride, serving as the undisputed centerpiece at gatherings and the subject of friendly regional rivalries across West Africa.

Ingredients

  • Long-grain parboiled rice
  • Bouillon cubes (seasoning cubes)
  • Scotch bonnet peppers (atarodo)
  • Plum tomatoes
  • Dried thyme
  • Red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • salt
  • Curry powder
  • Tomato paste
  • Butter
  • Bay leaves
  • Meat stock (chicken or beef)
  • Vegetable oil
  • Onions

Ordered list of the INGREDIENTS

  1. 3 cups Long-grain Parboiled Rice (or Basmati rice)
  2. 5 medium Fresh Tomatoes
  3. 3 Red Bell Peppers (Tatashe)
  4. 3 Scotch Bonnet Peppers (Atarodo)
  5. 2 medium Red Onions (1 sliced for frying, 1 blended)
  6. ½ cup Vegetable Oil
  7. 1 small can Tomato Paste (approx. 4 tablespoons)
  8. 3–4 cups Rich Chicken or Beef Stock
  9. 3 Bay Leaves
  10. 1 tablespoon Curry Powder
  11. 1 tablespoon Dried Thyme
  12. 2-3 Seasoning Cubes
  13. 2 tablespoons Butter (optional, for gloss and flavor)
  14. Salt to taste

  • 1. Prep the Base and Rice Blend the pepper mix: Roughly chop the tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet peppers, and one onion. Blend everything together until smooth. Pour the mixture into a pot and boil it down on medium-high heat until most of the water evaporates and you are left with a thick paste.
  • Wash the rice: Properly rinse your rice multiple times in warm water to remove excess surface starch (this keeps the grains from sticking together). Drain completely and set aside.
  • 2. Fry the Stew Base Sauté the aromatics: Heat the vegetable oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and fry for about 2–3 minutes until translucent. Stir in the thyme, curry powder, and bay leaves to wake up the spices in the hot oil.
  • Fry the tomato paste: Add the canned tomato paste and fry for 5 minutes, stirring constantly. It should darken slightly and lose its sour metallic taste.
  • Cook the blended pepper mix: Pour in your boiled-down pepper blend. Fry the mixture on medium heat for 10–12 minutes, stirring frequently, until the oil completely separates and rises to the top.
  • 3. Steam and Simmer the Rice Season the stock: Pour your rich meat stock into the fried stew base. Add your seasoning cubes and salt to taste. Bring the liquid to a rolling boil. Crucial tip:
  • The liquid should taste just a tiny bit salty because the rice will absorb a lot of it.
  • Add the rice: Stir in your washed rice until every grain is coated in the red sauce. The liquid should just barely cover the rice (about a quarter-inch above it). If it is too low, add a splash of warm water.Trapped steam method: Turn the heat down to very low. Cover the pot tightly with a layer of aluminum foil or a clean pouch bag, then slam the heavy lid on top. Jollof rice needs to be cooked by steam, not by boiling water.
  • The wait: Cook undisturbed for 20–25 minutes. Open and gently stir from the bottom up using a wooden spoon or spatula.The smokiness: Let the bottom burn just a little bit during the last 5 minutes of cooking. That scorched bottom is what releases the authentic smoky party aroma into the rest of the rice. Stir in the butter for a glossy finish, cover for 2 more minutes, and turn off the heat.
