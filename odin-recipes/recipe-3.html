Egusi Soup Recipe

Nigerian Egusi Soup is a rich, savory, and deeply satisfying West African staple made from ground melon seeds, red palm oil, leafy greens, and assorted meats or fish. A true cornerstone of Nigerian home cooking, it is famous for its thick, beautifully curded or creamy texture and its deeply nutty flavor profile. It is traditionally paired with swallows like pounded yam, eba (garri), or fufu.

Ingredients

  • Red Palm Oil
  • Ground Crayfish
  • Assorted Meats (Beef, goat meat, tripe/shaki, or cow skin/kpomo)
  • Scotch Bonnet Peppers (Atarodo)
  • Stockfish or Dried Fish
  • Salt
  • Egusi (ground melon seeds)
  • Chopped Leafy Greens (Fresh Ugu/pumpkin leaves, spinach, or washed bitterleaf)
  • Seasoning Cubes
  • Meat Stock
  • Red Onion

Ordered List Of The Ingredients

  1. 2 cups Egusi (ground melon seeds)
  2. ½ cup Red Palm Oil
  3. 1 lb Assorted Meats (Beef, goat meat, tripe/shaki, or cow skin/kpomo)
  4. 1 piece Stockfish or Dried Fish (soaked and cleaned)
  5. 2 tablespoons Ground Crayfish
  6. 1 medium Red Onion (half chopped for steaming meat, half chopped for the soup base)
  7. 2 Scotch Bonnet Peppers (Atarodo), blended or finely minced
  8. 2 Seasoning Cubes
  9. 2–3 cups Chopped Leafy Greens (Fresh Ugu/pumpkin leaves, spinach, or washed bitterleaf)
  10. Salt to taste
  11. 3–4 cups Meat Stock (reserved from cooking your meats)

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Prep the Proteins & Make the Paste Pre-cook the meat: In a large pot, combine your assorted meats, half of the chopped onion, 1 seasoning cube, and a pinch of salt. Add a little water and steam on medium heat until tender. Add the soaked stockfish or dried fish during the last 10–15 minutes so it softens without disintegrating. Save the meat stock!
  2. Make the egusi paste: While the meat cooks, mix the ground egusi seeds in a small bowl with a few tablespoons of warm water or meat stock until it forms a thick, moldable paste. Set aside.
  3. Fry the Base & Cook the Egusi Lumps Sauté the aromatics: Heat the red palm oil in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat for about 2 minutes (do not let it smoke or bleach). Toss in the remaining chopped onions and sauté for 2–3 minutes until fragrant. Stir in the blended scotch bonnet peppers and fry for another 3 minutes.
  4. Form the curds: Scoop the egusi paste in small spoonfuls directly into the hot oil mixture.
  5. Crucial tip: Do not stir immediately. Cover the pot and let the egusi fry undisturbed on medium-low heat for 5–7 minutes so it develops a firm, lumpy texture.Simmer the seeds: Gently flip or turn the lumps so they fry evenly for another 5 minutes. You want the egusi to look slightly toasted and firm.
  6. Simmer and Finish Build the soup: Pour in the reserved meat stock a little at a time, gently stirring to break down the massive lumps into smaller, bite-sized curds. Add your cooked meats, stockfish, ground crayfish, and the remaining seasoning cube.
  7. The long simmer: Cover the pot and let the soup simmer on medium-low heat for 15–20 minutes. Egusi must be thoroughly cooked; you will know it is ready when the orange-red palm oil begins to separate and float to the surface. If the soup is too thick, loosen it with a splash of water.
  8. Fold in the greens: Add your chopped ugu or spinach. Stir gently, let it simmer for just 3–5 minutes until the greens wilt but stay vibrant, and remove from the heat.Serve hot with freshly pounded yam, eba, or amala!
